One field, two ways of seeing

Cephalopods change their appearance by expanding and contracting tiny pigment organs called chromatophores. Here, every cell keeps its own size while following a shared pattern.

Chromatophore View reveals the individual organs. Skin View blends their coverage into an approximation of the whole surface. Switching views keeps the same movement running.

How to explore

Choose a pattern, then try a dynamic display. Increase the response rates for sharper movement, or lower them to see the cells lag behind their instructions.

Response delay makes the cells follow an earlier signal. Display speed changes the instructions, not the speed of the cells themselves.

Hold stops movement at the current sizes. Selecting a display again starts a new sequence at 1×; it does not resume the old sequence.

Keyboard shortcuts work while focus is inside the simulator, except in form inputs. Patterns: U, B, H, V, S, M, G. Displays: W, P, F, N, L, T. Expand: E; contract: C; randomise: R. Keys 1–3 cycle response settings; 4 cycles display speed. Use the Hold button to stop. Tab navigates normally.

An exploratory model inspired by cephalopods, not a biological reconstruction. Pigments occupy alternating positions; skin blending approximates combined appearance rather than physical skin optics.
